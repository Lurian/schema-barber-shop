# Components compose in the schema

A page is assembled in its page document, not inside components. Anything a page author could reasonably want to place, reorder, relabel, wrap, or leave out is its own catalog component, and the page document nests it. A component owns one behavior or one piece of data; it does not decide what sits next to it.

Shared state lives in root components that render no markup of their own: `ShopRoot` holds the shop, `ClientRoot` and `BarberRoot` hold a selection, `AddBookingDrawer` and `EditHoursDrawer` hold a draft, `Drawer` holds open state. Their children read that state through React context. A catalog entry names what it provides (`providesContext`) and what it needs (`requiresContext`), and the compiler rejects a page where a consumer has no providing ancestor. Placement is therefore checked at compile time; a component reads its context and adds no placement check of its own.

A list whose items an author may want to shape repeats its authored children once per item. `BookingList` renders its children for every booking, inside a row context that `ClientName`, `BarberName`, `BookingTime`, and `CancelButton` read, so each page decides what a row shows and in what order. A new element tree is mounted per row, so a `Drawer` inside a row opens that row's panel only. `ScopeClient` and `ScopeBarber` point a nested `BookingList` at the row's client or barber, which is how the barber page's "View" panel lists one client's bookings without a component written for it.

The rejected alternative was configurable components: an `AddBookingDrawer` that rendered its own button, title, and fields from `label` and `title` props, and a booking list that rendered its own rows. That is where this project started. Each new arrangement needed a new prop or a new component, the author could not move a button or drop a field, and the look was fixed by markup the schema could not see. Composition moves those choices into the document, where a human or an agent edits them without touching source. It is also what makes [ADR 0001](0001-schema-owns-component-css.md) workable: a part the schema cannot place is usually a part it cannot style.

The cost is size. A page document is long (`pages/client.json` spells out every drawer part and every row element), and the catalog has many small components rather than a few large ones. A component is also useless outside its provider, which the compiler reports rather than the component.

The rule currently stops at lists of plain choices. The pickers (`ClientPicker`, `BarberSelect`, `BookingBarber`), `BookingSlot`, and `HoursList` render their options internally, because an option is one button whose content comes from data the page cannot name. They expose a `data-ui-*` hook on every part so the schema still styles them. Once an author needs to compose an option, such as adding an avatar to a barber, that list follows `BookingList` and repeats authored children.
